About this demo

What it shows

The model

Vidal de Negreiros VLCC, fully loaded (L = 320 m), held by a hawser of fixed length. Water forces: Leite et al. (1998) with the yaw-rate terms of Simos (2001). Every frame here was computed in Python (fishtail/model_rigid.py) and is only played back, so the page shows exactly what the report shows.

How to read it

  • Top view. The buoy is the ringed dot; the current comes from the right. The streaks show the direction of the current, not its speed. Every run starts from the same small nudge (hawser 3° to port, bow 1° to port). In the Pitchfork tab you can nudge the ship to starboard instead, or put it on an angled rest position; those buttons are greyed out forward of the pitchfork point, where the angled positions do not exist. In the Wind tab a greyed-out button means that branch does not exist for this wind.
  • Control tabs. Every run starts while the ship fishtails fully (the swing without control at this friction) and the control starts at t* = 0. Tug: a hold-back tug made fast at the stern pulls steadily downstream (amber block and arrow). It is the wind force of the Wind tab, coming from ahead and acting at the stern. Once the bow swings off, the pull gets a sideways part that turns it back (a weathervane tail) and it adds to the hawser tension. Thruster: the tanker's own stern thruster pushes sideways (amber arrow, dashed when at its limit), N = −kp ψ − kd r in units of ½ρU²L²T: kp is a spring back to straight, kd a damper on the turning. In the time trace the amber line is the thruster force and the dotted amber lines are the limit. "Swing dying out" means the swing is still visible at the end of the shown run but decays (checked by running on for 800 t*). Thrust limit, tug pull range and the stern positions are PLACEHOLDERs (decision D31); forces in N (1:90 model) and kN (real ship) are given where a current speed gives this friction.
  • Phase plane. A path that spirals into a dot: the ship settles there. A path that winds onto a closed loop: a limit cycle, i.e. fishtailing. It is a 2-D view of a 4-D motion, so the path may cross itself.
  • Eigenvalues. Each rest position has four eigenvalues λ, one per state (γ, ψ and their rates), so three rest positions (Wind tab: port, starboard, middle) give twelve. A small disturbance changes like eλ t*: the real part Re λ is the growth rate, the imaginary part Im λ = ω says how fast it swings (period 2π/ω; ω = 0 means no swinging). Right of the vertical line is the right half-plane (RHP), Re λ > 0: there a disturbance grows, so a rest position with any λ in the RHP is unstable. A bifurcation happens when an eigenvalue crosses the line, and how it crosses decides which one: a real λ passing through 0 (▲) means the ship drifts off without swinging and finds new rest positions, a pitchfork; a complex pair crossing at ±ω (●) means the disturbance grows while it swings and levels off on a limit cycle, a Hopf bifurcation, i.e. fishtailing. In the Wind tab a real λ of the middle position and one of a side position close in on 0 from both sides as the force nears the fold (◇), where those two positions meet and vanish. One λ of every rest position is far to the left (below −1.7 U/L, gone within half a t*); it never decides stability, so it is only listed in the table ("fast λ"), not plotted. Mirror-image rest positions (port and starboard with no sideways wind) have the same λ, so their markers lie on top of each other and they share one row of the table. Grey dots: the same rest positions at the other slider values (Wind tab: the other forces from this direction), so you can see the path the λ follow as you move the slider.
  • Time trace. Top: hawser angle γ (blue) and heading ψ (green). Bottom: hawser force FH/FH0 (violet), the tension divided by its value when the ship trails straight without wind (dotted line = 1).
  • Time t*. Measured in the time the current needs to pass one ship length, t* = U t / L. For the real ship in a 1 m/s current one unit is 320 s, about 5 minutes. Playback: at 1× one second on screen is ten units of t* (½× to 4×). The line under the speed menu says how long one unit of t* lasts in real time, t = t* L / U: for the 1:90 tank model and for the real ship, each at the current speed that gives this friction coefficient CF (the real ship only where such a speed exists: at the friction values of the Pitchfork, Hawser-length and Wind tabs it would need a current slower than 1 cm/s). Real-time playback is not offered because one unit of t* lasts from seconds to hours.
